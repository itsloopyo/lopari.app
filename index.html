01 What it does

Every head-tracking game you own, in one place.

Lopari is a launcher first. You open it, you see your library — but filtered: only the games installed on this machine that can be played with head tracking are shown. Everything else stays out of the way.

For most of that list, head tracking is something the developer shipped. Elite Dangerous, DCS World, Microsoft Flight Simulator, iRacing, Assetto Corsa, Star Citizen, IL-2, X-Plane — all of them read your tracker out of the box. Lopari gathers the connection details in one place, instead of you hunting through each game's options menu.

For the handful of titles where tracking isn't shipped natively, a community mod exists that adds it. Lopari installs that mod for you on first launch, and removes it again when you want vanilla back. Same files you'd touch by hand — one click away, and reversible.

02 How to use it

Surfaces your games. Launches them. Done.

  1. 01

    It finds the games

    Open Lopari. It surfaces every game you already own that works with head tracking — some natively, some through a community mod.

  2. 02

    You launch in one click

    Hit play. Lopari opens the game through your store, installing the mod first when one's needed and removing it again when you're done.

  3. 03

    You bring the tracking

    You'll still need something that tracks your head — a webcam, a phone, whatever you've got. Lopari surfaces and launches; the rest is up to you.

03 Get it

Public beta. Windows only.

Lopari is in public beta — Windows 10 and Windows 11 only. macOS and Linux are not supported.

Heads-up: the installer isn't code-signed yet, so Windows will warn you about an unrecognised publisher. Nothing is wrong — it's expected for a small independent project that hasn't bought a certificate. The steps below tell you exactly what to click, and a SHA256 digest ships with every release so you can verify the bundle yourself.

Installing on Windows

  1. Download the installer.

    Grab the -setup.exe from the latest release.

  2. If your browser warns about the download, keep it.

    Chrome and Edge sometimes label unsigned downloads as "uncommon" or "may harm your device." Click the dropdown next to the warning and choose Keep (Chrome) or Keep anyway (Edge).

  3. Unblock the file.

    Windows tags every file downloaded from the internet so it can refuse to run it later. Right-click the downloaded .exe → Properties → at the bottom of the General tab, tick Unblock → OK. Some Windows installs skip straight to step 4; do it anyway, it doesn't hurt.

  4. Run the installer.

    Double-click the .exe. SmartScreen shows a blue warning that says Windows protected your PC and lists the publisher as Unknown. Click More info, then Run anyway. The installer takes a few seconds and Lopari appears in your Start menu.

  5. If Windows Defender quarantines it.

    Occasionally Defender flags unsigned Tauri installers heuristically. Open Windows Security → Virus & threat protection → Protection history, find the Lopari entry, and choose Restore. Then re-run step 4. The published SHA256 is your independent check that the bundle matches what was built.

When Lopari is code-signed, steps 2, 3, and 4 will go away.
